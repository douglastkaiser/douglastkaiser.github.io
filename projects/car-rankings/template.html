How scoring works

Scoring method

×

Equal evidence earns equal scores

Cost and Eco order their underlying assessments across all $count cars, placing tied values by midpoint percentile: (number strictly better + half the tied group) / cohort count. Bands at 20%, 40%, 60% and 80% receive +2, +1, 0, −1 and −2; exact boundaries go toward zero. These two categories remain relative, and adding a candidate can change them. Trips, Comfort, Assistance and Upkeep instead use fixed component-composite bands: below 1.5/5 → −2; 1.5 to below 2.5 → −1; 2.5 to below 3.5 → 0; 3.5 to below 4.5 → +1; 4.5 and above → +2. The 3/5 workable or uncertain baseline therefore stays neutral. Category ranks still show raw-composite ordering with shared ties; they are distinct from score bands. These thresholds are an editorial rubric, not calibrated performance measurements.

Total = sum of each category score × its allocated points. Each active category adds one point to the shared budget; turning it off removes one point. Sliders run from off to two points (twice the default), with one point at the center. Changing an active slider redistributes the remainder proportionally, capping every category at two points and sharing any excess among the others. Every category can be turned off. Five categories start at one point each; Maintenance starts off. Tiers remain fixed while allocations change. Equal totals sort by lower five-year cost, then name. With all categories off, totals are zero; the default Total sort breaks ties by cost. Click any column to sort its displayed values and click again to reverse. Scores sort high to low first; Vehicle and # sort ascending first. Equal category values break by weighted Total, then lower five-year cost and name. Sorting does not change the weighted rank (#), leader, scores or Pareto paths. Sorting persists when sliders change. Reset restores weights and Total descending.

Every car receives a numeric assessment for every metric. Where exact evidence is incomplete, the best available proxy or editorial estimate is used, with its basis in the details. Zero means middle band for Cost/Eco and workable or uncertain for the capability categories. Uncertainty does not introduce a separate bonus or penalty.

Pareto paths

A direct upgrade is no worse on every active tier and better on at least one, with no intermediate score profile between the two. Equal profiles share a node. Each layer removes the remaining non-dominated profiles. The preferred path repeatedly chooses the lowest-scoring direct dominator; equal totals break by calculated five-year cost, then name. This tie-break is display order, not an additional dominance metric.

All $count cars participate, including those with proxy estimates. Rankings, Pareto comparisons and research dialogs use the same five-level scores, using the disclosed relative or fixed rubric. Positive weight changes cannot change dominance; turning a metric on or off can. More levels expose more tradeoffs, not more precise evidence.

Ownership: one visible five-year calculation

New dealer-purchased EVs are exempt from the $$121 Road Improvement Fee at initial registration; the four renewals include it. Pickup weight fees are included as explicit annual bracket allowances; exact registered weight needs a dealer/DMV quote. Five-year costs are scenarios: resale, insurance, fuel rates and charging prices can change the order. See the full scoring audit and sensitivity table.

New vehicles of any body style, estimated at or below $$60,000 out the door in Orange County. Prices use the named model year and configuration, published MSRP, destination and selected packages. No assumed dealer discount, markup or contingent rebate. MSRP in the breakdown excludes destination so it is added exactly once. A published US configuration is not a claim of current stock at a particular dealer. Model years may overlap during the sales year.

Five-year cost = OTD − resale + gasoline + electricity + four registration renewals + insurance + routine upkeep + repair reserve + selected assistance subscriptions. Financing is excluded. First registration and sales tax appear once, inside OTD. Roof carriers and winter tires are extra for every vehicle; no carrier purchase is assumed in the base cost.

Common scenario: $annual_miles miles/year for five years; $tax_rate% Irvine sales tax baseline (other OC cities differ); $$$document_fee taxable document fee; current DMV registration/CHP/TIF/VLF schedules plus a $$$county_fee annual county-fee allowance. Renewal value factors are 90%, 80%, 70%, 60% of purchase price, explicit planning approximations rather than a DMV calculation. EV road fees are included. Rates are held constant for this comparison.

Nearby public charging is assumed available at ordinary local electricity cost, $$$electricity_rate/kWh, with no installation charge, availability penalty or retail charging markup, as requested. This is a comparison assumption, not a posted public-station tariff. Gasoline is held at the checked OC AAA reference: $$$regular_rate regular / $$$premium_rate premium per gallon. The EPA record’s fuel grade is costed; premium-fuel references do not establish that regular is prohibited. EPA wall-energy consumption already includes charging losses; none are added twice.

PHEVs use a disclosed 60% electric-mile scenario and 40% gasoline miles; this assumes regular access to charging and is not an EPA prediction for your driving. Gas-mode MPG and electrical consumption are separate. Resale uses the identified historical model or segment depreciation benchmark; new generations/powertrains use segment assumptions, not invented exact-build forecasts. The applied fraction and its scope appear in each Cost assessment.

Insurance is a common $$$insurance_year/year allowance, not a personalized or model-specific quote. Routine upkeep allowances are $gas_upkeep¢/mile gas, $hybrid_upkeep¢ hybrid/PHEV, and $ev_upkeep¢ EV, including tires. Repairs add a common $$$repair_year/year reserve. These round planning inputs are not measured 2026 expenses; the DOE study supports the maintenance-cost distinction, not these exact allowances. Resale and insurance are substantial uncertainties; compare quotes and sensitivity before a purchase.

Comfort includes the liftgate

40% ride/noise + 30% seating + 20% cabin ergonomics + 10% assessed power liftgate. Each component is 0–5; a selected power liftgate assessed as present earns 5 on that component and a manual opening earns 0. Automatic trunk unlatching alone is not a powered liftgate. Body style and power liftgate never determine eligibility. Comfort does not include driver assistance.

Ride, noise, seats and controls use qualitative road-test findings where applicable, with four explicit editorial rationales per car. These are model-level proxies, often based on a different trim, wheel size or carryover test year. They are not reproduced publisher ratings, instrumented comparisons under identical conditions or exact selected-build measurements. No upper-grade seating, damping or comfort option is automatically borrowed.

Where findings do not transfer convincingly, retain 3/5 as a workable or uncertain baseline. Clear seat-space, noise and control drawbacks can lower components; published praise can support provisional improvements. A brand badge, upholstery, price or EV propulsion alone earns no bonus. Contradictory or dated reviews are identified rather than forced into precise ratings. At 10% of Comfort, a powered hatch cannot by itself move an otherwise baseline cabin into a positive band. Test the actual build and all three adults.

Eco: historical lifecycle anchors and best estimates

ACEEE Green Scores consider manufacturing, energy supply, use and disposal impacts. Published US coverage ends at model year 2024. $historical_count retained assessments use comparable historical configurations and preserve their original source records. They are historical comparison anchors, not measured current-model results.

The other $proxy_count assessments are editorial lifecycle proxies on the same 0–100 comparison scale. Prefer a historical configuration in the same model family and powertrain, then a similar vehicle class; configuration differences remain explicit limitations rather than unsupported numerical adjustments. Each detail names the proxy models, their published scores and the adjustment rationale. Redesigns and different models receive estimates rather than purported exact published ratings.

The ownership-cost assumption of 60% electric PHEV miles does not recalculate a historical lifecycle score. Charging habits affect real-world impacts. No direct MPG-versus-MPGe comparison or mixing of European Green NCAP scores is used. Efficient use-phase consumption alone is not a full lifecycle assessment. Proxy values incorporate judgment and remain approximate; five-level tiers do not imply additional measurement precision.

Mountain trips: three adults and their gear

40% carrying usefulness + 35% winter access + 25% trip practicality. Assess two front occupants, one rear adult, bags and long ski/snowboard gear. Maximum cargo with every rear seat folded does not prove fit. Sedan access and low clearance affect usefulness; AWD alone does not prove good winter tires or stopping ability.

Carrying estimates use the manufacturer’s packaging evidence, with verified dimensions where available. Gear fit is unmeasured. Roof routes remain conditional on exact roof, bars/feet, load rating, carrier capacity and clearance. Roof loads add drag, height and handling/loading costs. Current EV range and charging hardware inform trip estimates, without claiming an exact cold-weather or roof-load range penalty.

Trip practicality uses a provisional 3/5 EV baseline until a sourced assessment of loaded winter range, arrival reserve and charging stops supports a higher input. EPA range or peak charging power alone does not establish that upgrade. EVs can earn higher inputs with that evidence; there is no fixed EV tier cap. Gas, hybrid and PHEV builds receive 5/5 for rapid liquid-fuel replenishment, not for overall trip excellence. Carrying and winter access are separate. No exact winter-range percentage or charging wait is assumed.

Carrying scores are packaging estimates, not verified ski/board fit. Winter access reflects the selected drivetrain and clearance; AWD does not establish braking grip. A +2 describes a strong rubric fit, not verified suitability for a particular winter route.

Commute assistance: selected equipment, not options you did not buy

40% traffic/speed control + 35% steering coverage + 25% driver supervision. Each selected system has its own component rationale. Traffic/speed 4 means ordinary stop/go following; 5 adds supported route-aware speed or intersection handling. Steering 0 means corrective support only, 3 means speed-limited centering, 4 means ordinary continuous centering, and 5 adds assisted lane changes or route turns. Driver supervision 3 is the hands-on baseline; 4 credits documented attention-monitoring or coordinated driver-inactivity response. These are editorial capability estimates, not independent performance scores. A lane-departure warning or corrective lane-keeping system receives no continuous-centering credit. Execution quality remains unscored: intermittent success, annoying alerts and intervention frequency are not measured by equipment presence. Read the linked road tests and test the selected system. A manual describing optional equipment does not prove trim fitment. Where fitment evidence is incomplete, use the best estimate for the selected grade and explain it in the details; optional equipment is not automatically credited.

Costs include selected assistance options and any credited paid subscription over five years; temporary trials do not establish permanent entitlement. Unselected hands-free upgrades receive no credit. Honda/Acura lane-centering operating speeds, restart behavior and other system limits remain important. These are capability estimates, not independent performance or safety ratings; IIHS safeguards ratings are not steering-quality scores.

Maintenance evidence and uncertainty

50% fewer disruptions + 30% less routine service effort + 20% service access. This is a low-confidence editorial comparison and starts off. Evidence confidence is shown separately and never enters the calculation. Without applicable failure evidence, every vehicle uses the same 3/5 disruption baseline; neither a redesign nor a new powertrain is penalized, and age or brand reputation alone earns no reliability bonus. Service access also uses a shared 3/5 baseline without a local shop census. Gas, hybrid and PHEV routine effort shares a 3/5 baseline because comparable schedule/time evidence has not established a difference; no PHEV complexity penalty is applied. EVs receive 5/5 routine effort for fewer scheduled engine-related tasks in the DOE framework, while tires, brakes and other service remain. These are disclosed estimates, not exact-build maintenance schedules.

Dollar service and repair allowances are already in Cost. This category emphasizes time, effort and disruption, so consider overlap when weighting it. RepairPal describes a historical methodology; the components here are not RepairPal ratings, a local shop census, failure probabilities or numeric repairability scores.

Editorial component rubric

0 = assessed as absent; 1 = very constrained; 2 = constrained; 3 = workable baseline; 4 = useful capability with caveats; 5 = unusually strong fit. Each assessment shows its inputs and rationale. Estimated fitment is identified with its evidence and rationale; it is distinct from verified equipment. Capability components describe planning usefulness, not laboratory results or failure rates. No hidden confidence bonus or penalty is applied.
